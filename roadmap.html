Named capability dependencies

I1 Google Cloud data platform → I5 Augmedix clinical documentation and I6 Nurse handoff assistant: supplies approved, traceable clinical data and secure access. This is our proposed common architecture, not a claim that every vendor uses the same backend.

I2 EHR integration and workflow standardization → I5 Augmedix clinical documentation: validates the interface for writing clinician-approved notes into the record. I2 → I6 Nurse handoff assistant: validates patient matching, access to current records and the local handoff workflow. Without I2, neither pilot can enter integrated production under our proposed release rules. This is a readiness dependency, not a claim that either tool technically requires Expanse.

I3 Responsible AI Program → I4 Timpani nurse scheduling, I5 Augmedix clinical documentation and I6 Nurse handoff assistant: supplies named oversight, human review and controlled model changes.

I4-W → I4-R: proves usable schedules and less preparation work. I5-W → I5-R: proves documentation quality and time benefit. I6-W → I6-R: proves handoff completeness and adoption. Each transition requires the gates on the next page.

Crawl parallelism: I1, I2 and I3 start together. I1 qualifies the secure data platform; I2 tests local record interfaces using test records and existing approved environments; I3 defines oversight. None requires another Crawl initiative to finish. Enterprise Expanse conversion is outside the nine-month readiness scope.

Pilot gates and scale requirements

All thresholds on this page are proposed classroom planning assumptions. Establish a four-week local baseline before each pilot and compare comparable units, workloads and shifts. Clinical leaders must approve the safety criteria before use.

I4 Timpani nurse scheduling

Walk scope: two nursing units in each of three hospitals for a 12-week evaluation. Crawl dependency: I3 oversight. Separate entry requirements: validated workforce and timekeeping feeds, staff skill profiles and approved scheduling rules. No direct dependency on I2 or Expanse is asserted. Readiness outcome: at least 20% less schedule-preparation time than baseline, without worse staffing-rule compliance. At least 80% of eligible nurse managers must actively use the tool.

Run scope: expand I4-W across eligible nursing units in all participating HCA hospital divisions, in waves. Required organizational change: a common scheduling policy, trained local nurse-manager champions, a workforce-data owner and a formal path for local exceptions. A new site enters only after its own readiness checks.

I5 Augmedix clinical documentation

Walk scope: approximately 75 volunteer ER physicians across four hospitals, using the historical pilot’s scale as a planning reference. Dependencies: I1 secure data access, I2 tested write-back of approved notes and I3 clinical oversight. Readiness outcome: at least 20% less documentation time per encounter over 12 weeks, with no deterioration in audited note quality and physician approval of every final note.

Run scope: expand I5-W to eligible emergency departments across HCA. Required organizational change: department-level training, a physician owner for note quality, protected training time and ongoing chart audits. Expansion beyond emergency care needs a separate evaluation and is not assumed here.

I6 Nurse handoff assistant

Walk scope: two inpatient units at one hospital for 12 weeks, using the published single-hospital test as a scope reference. Dependencies: I1 clinical data, I2 validated patient matching and current-record access, and I3 review controls. Readiness outcome: at least 15% less handoff-preparation time, no deterioration in audited completeness, and nurse review of every generated handoff.

Run scope: expand I6-W across eligible inpatient nursing units in participating HCA divisions. Required organizational change: a standard handoff checklist, nurse educators, shift-level training and escalation rules. The tool supports, rather than replaces, nurse-to-nurse communication.

Pause and release decisions

An unresolved critical safety or privacy incident pauses the affected pilot or rollout wave. Missed benefit or adoption targets trigger a review before additional funding. Passing a time-saving target never overrides a clinical safety finding. These are proposed governance rules, not claims about HCA’s existing policy.

Phase rationales

Crawl rationale

Crawl prepares selected sites for integrated production. I1 provides approved data access, I2 validates record interfaces and workflows, and I3 establishes oversight. These workstreams can begin in parallel; I2 can use test records before the shared platform is complete. Without I2, I5 lacks tested note write-back and I6 lacks validated access to current patient records, so both fail our proposed production gate. Timpani instead needs workforce feeds and scheduling rules. The nine-month foundation covers local readiness, while the broader Expanse migration continues separately. Walk starts only after the relevant checks pass.

Walk rationale

Walk tests whether the tools help real staff in a controlled setting. Timpani, clinical documentation and nurse handoff use the foundation differently, so each needs its own baseline, limited deployment and measured result. A favorable demonstration is not enough. The pilots must show less administrative work without weaker clinical quality or staffing compliance, and users must actually adopt the tools. These checks justify later investment and reveal training or workflow changes while the exposure is still small. Only a successful pilot moves into its corresponding Run rollout.

Run rationale

Run expands proven deployments rather than introducing untested use cases. Each rollout inherits its pilot’s evidence, but a new hospital still needs local data, training and safety checks. Enterprise standards create consistency, while an exception process recognizes that hospitals and units differ. Scaling therefore requires organizational changes: local champions, clear ownership, continuing audits and support for staff. We release investment in waves and pause when quality, adoption or benefit targets are missed. This prevents a large installation count from being mistaken for a successful transformation.

What this roadmap does not claim

The proposed phases are a dependency-based interpretation of published initiatives, not HCA’s historical calendar. Published pilot or rollout counts demonstrate activity, not proven financial or clinical outcomes. We do not use the unverified September 2026 hospital count, assume cost savings, or describe Run as already complete.

Why the sequence matters

The central risk is scaling a useful-looking tool before local data, staff adoption and clinical review are ready. The practical response is to fund readiness first, collect evidence in a limited setting, and give leaders explicit stop or expand decisions at every gate.
